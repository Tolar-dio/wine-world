# Burgundy premier cru climats (and the Corton climats): profiles for Vitis Mundi

Deliverable written alongside these notes: `/home/claude/winesite/data/content/depth_burgundy_pc.json`, 678 `appellations` profiles keyed by the fine-unit `id` of every Premier Cru and Corton Grand Cru polygon in `site/data/geo/fr_fine.geojson` (654 premier cru units across 32 appellations from Chablis and Fixin to Pouilly-Vinzelles, plus 24 Corton climats). About 120 profiles run 120 to 270 words (the celebrated climats) and about 50 more run 100 to 119; the rest 40 to 100. Total about 52,700 words. Build pipeline and data provenance: `/home/claude/winesite/work/depth-burgundy_pc-log.md`.

## Key question 1: for each climat, what are its size, exposure, elevation, soils, permitted colours, relation to neighbours, producers and monopoles, and history?

### Takeaway
Size, commune split, exposure, elevation band, gradient and neighbours were measured for all 661 units from primary geodata (the INAO parcel delimitation and the Copernicus 30 m terrain model), so every profile carries hard numbers even where nothing else is documented. History, ownership and monopoles were verified on the web for the climats where errors were most likely (reclassification debates, recent ownership changes, new premiers crus); style descriptions and most producer lists rest on expert background knowledge and should be treated as such.

### Inferences
- The INAO delimited areas differ slightly from the planted areas usually quoted (Les Amoureuses 5.28 ha delimited vs 5.4 ha commonly published); the profiles give the INAO figure as `area_ha` and mention the published figure where it differs.
- Umbrella dénominations matter: in Chassagne (Morgeot, La Boudriotte, Les Brussonnes, Bois de Chassagne, Cailleret, Clos Saint-Jean, La Grande Montagne, La Maltroie, Les Chenevottes, Les Vergers), Saint-Aubin (Les Combes, Le Charmois, Sur le Sentier du Clou, Les Frionnes, Les Champlots, La Chatenière, En Remilly, Sur Gamay) and elsewhere, a smaller climat may be sold under a larger name, so the same vines can appear under two or three label names. The profiles say so explicitly for each sub-climat.
- Twin units (same vines, two appellations): Blagny red premiers crus vs Meursault or Puligny white premiers crus on the same lieux-dits; Volnay-Santenots red vs Meursault white on Les Santenots Blancs, du Milieu and Les Plures; Corton climats vs Aloxe-Corton or Ladoix premiers crus of the same name on the lower slope. Profiles were written to distinguish the colour and appellation angle rather than repeat each other.
- The DEM confirms several orientation stories often told in prose: Fourchaume and Vosgros face west-southwest (afternoon sun), Mont de Milieu and Les Fourneaux southeast; Saint-Aubin's best sites (En Remilly, La Chatenière, Les Champlots) face southwest or west-southwest, away from the main Côte; Givry's Clos du Cellier aux Moines faces south-southwest; Cros Parantoux sits in a fold opening east to northeast.

### Gaps
- Style notes, most producer lists, and many ownership details for Côte d'Or climats come from background expert knowledge (the sort of material in Jasper Morris's Inside Burgundy and the Norman/Taylor domaine guide), not from a per-climat web check; they were cross-checked against neighbour geometry and softened or removed where uncertain. Producer lists for obscure climats were left empty rather than guessed.
- Etymologies are hedged ("probably", "usually said") because no single authoritative source was consulted; several speculative ones were removed.
- No reliable per-climat source was found for current ownership of: Gevrey La Romanée (formerly a Varoilles monopole; post-2020 owner unconfirmed), Mercurey Clos du Château de Montaigu, La Mission and Clos Tonnerre monopoly status, several Givry and Montagny holders; these are stated cautiously or not at all.
- Les Saint-Georges grand cru status as of October 2026: sources disagree on how far the INAO procedure has advanced; no decision was found.
- The 1:1M GISCO commune boundaries are too coarse for climat-level commune attribution and were not used; the INAO commune split was used instead.
- Chablis: 27 premier cru polygons are in the fine file. Ten come from INAO dénominations; 17 more (Montée de Tonnerre, Côte de Bréchain, Vaillons and six Vaillons satellites, Forêts, Côte de Léchet, Berdiot, L'Homme Mort, Morein, Vau Ligneau, Vau de Vey, Côte de Savant) were added by another agent from cadastral lieux-dits clipped to the INAO premier cru area, with a `vine_ha` figure used here as `area_ha`. Several principal names (Montmains, Beauroy, Chapelot, Butteaux, Vaulorent, Côte de Vaubarousse) still have no polygon and therefore no profile.
- Discrepancy: the new cadastral data gives Sécher's principal climat as Roncières and Roncières as a principal name, while the BIVB/Pitiot listing places both Sécher and Roncières under Vaillons — [Burgundy Report: Chablis](https://www.burgundy-report.com/ref/apps/chablis.html). The profiles follow the BIVB listing; the geodata `reg` field should be checked against the current cahier des charges (INAO extranet CDC-Chablis-250623).

## Key question 2: what is the regional texture (Côte de Nuits vs Côte de Beaune, Chablis premier crus and their satellites)?

### Takeaway
The profiles carry a consistent regional frame: Côte de Nuits premiers crus are almost all red, usually tucked above, below or between grands crus, and judged by how closely they echo their grand cru neighbours; Côte de Beaune premiers crus split between red villages (Beaune, Pommard, Volnay, Savigny, Santenay) and white villages (Meursault, Puligny, Chassagne, Saint-Aubin), with far larger premier cru areas and stronger umbrella-name systems; Chablis premiers crus are grouped into main names with satellites on Kimmeridgian slopes on both banks of the Serein; the Côte Chalonnaise and Mâconnais add younger or reformed premier cru maps (Montagny 1989 to 1991, Pouilly-Fuissé 2020, Loché and Vinzelles 2024).

### Cited Findings
- In Gevrey, 1930s grand cru status went only to vineyards bordering Chambertin and Clos de Bèze, leaving Clos Saint-Jacques premier cru — [Wikipedia: Clos Saint-Jacques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clos_Saint-Jacques)
- Pommard (Côte de Beaune) has no grand cru from the 1936 classification and its growers have sought promotion for Rugiens and Epenots — [Decanter](https://www.decanter.com/wine-news/pommard-producers-ask-inao-to-create-first-grand-crus-35938/)
- Nuits-Saint-Georges likewise has no grand cru; Les Saint-Georges is the candidate — [The Drinks Business](https://thedrinksbusiness.com/?p=568333)
- Meursault: 104.69 ha of premier cru out of 382.3 ha; red premier cru exists but under 3 ha — [Burgundy Report: AOC Meursault](https://www.burgundy-report.com/?p=93150)
- Montagny's 1943 alcohol-based premier cru rule and its 1989 to 1991 reform — [Decanter, Anson](https://www.decanter.com/wine-news/opinion/news-blogs-anson/anson-on-thursday-tasting-the-burgundy-climats-294569/)
- Pouilly-Fuissé's 2020 premiers crus are the first in the Mâconnais — [Decanter](https://www.decanter.com/wine-news/new-pouilly-fuisse-premier-crus-designations-444293/); Loché and Vinzelles followed for the 2024 vintage with the first herbicide ban in Bourgogne premier cru rules — [The Drinks Business](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2024/12/pouilly-loche-and-pouilly-vinzelles-aoc-win-new-premier-cru-sites)
- Chablis premier crus are grouped into principal names with satellites (Montée de Tonnerre with Chapelot, Pied d'Aloup and Côte de Bréchain; Fourchaume with Vaupulent, Côte de Fontenay, L'Homme Mort and Vaulorent; Vaillons with Châtains, Sécher, Beugnons, Les Lys, Mélinots, Roncières and Les Epinottes; Montmains with Forêt and Butteaux; Beauroy with Troesmes and Côte de Savant; Vau de Vey with Vaux Ragons; Vosgros with Vaugiraut; Les Fourneaux with Morein and Côte des Prés Girots; Les Beauregards with Côte de Cuissy); 747 ha of premier cru in production out of 3,425 ha (1999 figures) — [Burgundy Report: Chablis](https://www.burgundy-report.com/ref/apps/chablis.html)
- The INAO-derived Chablis premier cru polygons in the fine file are distributed over Fleys (Mont de Milieu, Les Fourneaux), Chichée (Vaucoupin, Vosgros, Vaugiraut), Courgis (Côte de Jouan, Les Beauregards, Chaume de Talvat) and the Fourchaume communes (Maligny, La Chapelle-Vaupelteigne, Fontenay-près-Chablis) — [INAO parcel delimitation, data.gouv.fr](https://www.data.gouv.fr/fr/datasets/5aaf6b7ec751df67b0d7a87f/)

### Inferences
- Measured from the INAO data, the 135 Côte de Nuits units are small (most 1 to 7 ha) and sit at roughly 240 to 390 m, while the Côte de Beaune has several premier crus above 25 ha (Beaune Grèves and Clos des Mouches, Meursault Charmes, Saint-Aubin En Remilly, Santenay Gravières, Maranges La Fussière) and umbrella names above 50 ha (Chassagne Morgeot).
- Côte de Nuits profiles lean on adjacency to grands crus (Aux Combottes islanded among four grands crus; Cherbaudes between Mazis and Chapelle; La Croix Rameau against Romanée-Saint-Vivant; Aux Malconsorts against La Tâche); Côte de Beaune profiles lean more on colour, umbrella names and the red/white split within a village (Chassagne, Santenots, Blagny).
- In Chablis, west-southwest-facing slopes (Fourchaume, Vosgros) give fuller wines than southeast-facing ones (Mont de Milieu, Les Fourneaux); this is stated as an inference in the profiles.

### Gaps
- No regional statistics source was used to give premier cru totals for every village (only Meursault, Monthélie, Ladoix, Montagny, Givry and Pouilly-Fuissé totals were found); the integrator should not rely on these profiles for village-level totals.
- Geological detail (Bajocian, Bathonian, Comblanchien, Prémeaux limestone, Kimmeridgian marl) is general Côte knowledge, not verified per climat against BRGM geological maps; a BRGM 1:50,000 overlay would be the natural next step.
