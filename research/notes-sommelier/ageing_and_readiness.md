# Wine ageing potential and vintage readiness (Vitis Mundi)

Deliverables (built 2026-10-29, status as of October 2026):
- `site/data/ageing/profiles.json`: 1,213 explicit appellation profiles with tiers, plus 699 `_defaults` keys (`<country>|<style>|<category>`, with `|<subcat>` for broad zones and `*` fallbacks), a `_fine_tier` map and a small `_fine_override` map. 1.1 MB.
- `site/data/ageing/vintages.json`: 41 chart entries, 1,433 vintage rows (1990 to 2024 plus legendary older years; 2025 added for some Southern Hemisphere regions), `_covers` (entry to appellation ids), `_by_app` (appellation id to entries, 465 ids), `_meta`. 0.46 MB.
- Build scripts and part files: `work/ageing/` (`build_profiles.py`, `build_vintages.py`, `plib.py`, `vlib.py`, `parts/p*.py` for profiles, `parts/v*.py` for vintages, `parts/v9*_fix*.py` for expanded notes, `url_check.tsv`, and `check_fine.py`, which resolves every fine unit to a profile and tier and prints coverage). Systembolaget charts parsed by `work/ageing/tools/parse_sb3.py` (run with `python3 -I`) from `raw/ageing_charts/sb/` into `work/ageing/sb_charts.json`.

## (A) How long does each appellation take to open and how long does it keep, by tier and style, and why?

### Takeaway
Every appellation in the atlas now resolves to an ageing estimate: 1,213 have hand-built, tiered profiles (all of France's 455 indexed AOCs including every Burgundy, Chablis and Alsace grand cru, 171 Italian, 117 US, 97 Spanish and the main New World regions), and the other 1,371 fall back to rule-based defaults by country, style and category. All 9,487 fine units (MGAs, UGAs, premiers crus, lieux-dits, Champagne cru villages, Rioja municipios, Priorat villages, Einzellagen, Rieden) resolve to a profile except one parentless Ried; 7,074 get a specific tier (cru, premier cru, single-site, GG, Ried) and 2,412 deliberately take the appellation's default tier. Tier numbers are my synthesis of standard references and charts rather than figures quoted from any one source.

### Cited Findings
- Legal ageing minimums that drive Rioja's tiers: Crianza 2 years (1 in oak), Reserva 3 (1 in oak), Gran Reserva 5 (2 in oak); the Consejo publishes official vintage ratings used to anchor the Rioja chart. [Consejo Regulador DOCa Rioja](https://riojawine.com/doca-rioja/anadas)
- Brunello is released five years after the vintage (Riserva six), which sets the "ready" floor for its tiers; the 2021s were released in 2026 and received as outstanding. [The Drinks Business, Brunello new releases, April 2026](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2026/04/highlights-of-the-new-releases-from-brunello-di-montalcino/)
- Tokaji Aszú longevity rests on botrytis concentration plus acidity; insiders single out high-acid years (2008, 2016, 2021) as the longest-lived. [bor.hu, the best years for Aszú](https://bor.hu/en/magazine/liquid-gold-the-best-years-for-aszu-is-this-year-one-of-them/)
- Madeira: frasqueira releases from 1987 and 1989 and colheitas from 2011 were bottled in 2024, the author arguing that Madeira "needs age" and frasqueira gives better value than young colheita. [Richard Mayson, Blandy's and Cossart Gordon releases 2024](https://richardmayson.com/madeira-wine-notes/new-releases-from-blandys-and-cossart-gordon-2024); enthusiasts stress that producer matters more than year. [For the Love of Port forum, Madeira great years](https://www.fortheloveofport.com/ftlopforum/viewtopic.php?p=17901)
- Port styles: wood-aged tawny and colheita are sold ready (years counted from bottling), while Vintage Port is bottled young and needs decades; single-quinta wines are released in non-declared years. [IVDP](https://www.ivdp.pt); declaration practice confirmed in the 2021 and 2024 threads. [Port Forum 2021](https://www.theportforum.com/viewtopic.php?p=129563), [Port Forum 2024](https://www.theportforum.com/viewtopic.php?p=142961)
- Swedish monopoly charts give storage guidance per region and were used as a cross-check on ratings and on the length of windows (for example Mendoza 2023 premium reds "potential for long storage"). [Systembolaget årgångstabell](https://www.systembolaget.se/vin/argang-och-lagring/argangstabell/argentina/mendoza/)
- Profile `why` and `producer_style` texts draw on standard reference knowledge (grape structure, oak, fortification, sugar, closures, premox, traditional versus modern Nebbiolo, Rioja and Sangiovese practice). [Oxford Companion to Wine, 5th ed.](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/the-oxford-companion-to-wine-9780198871316)

### Inferences
- Schema as built: `profiles["<id>"] = {tiers: {key: {label, ready, peak:[a,b], limit, basis?}}, default_tier, why, producer_style, src}`. `basis` is `bottling` (tawny Port, sherry, oxidative VDN, Madeira blends, Rutherglen Muscat, Marsala) or `disgorgement` (non-vintage sparkling); otherwise years count from the vintage. `limit` means the outer limit for good examples stored well, not the typical bottle.
- Key tiers by appellation (ready / peak / limit, years from vintage): Barolo normale 8 / 10 to 25 / 45, MGA cru 10 / 12 to 30 / 50, Riserva 12 / 15 to 35 / 60. Barbaresco normale 5 / 7 to 18 / 32, cru 7 / 10 to 22 / 40, Riserva 9 / 12 to 28 / 50. Médoc classed growth 10 / 15 to 35 / 50, first growth 15 / 20 to 45 / 70. Côte de Nuits premier cru 7 / 10 to 20 / 30; top red grand crus 10 / 15 to 35 / 50. Meursault premier cru 5 / 6 to 15 / 22. Chablis premier cru 3 / 5 to 12 / 20, grand cru 5 / 8 to 20 / 28. Sauternes classed growth 6 / 10 to 40 / 80. Champagne vintage 7 / 10 to 25 / 40, prestige 10 / 12 to 30 / 50. Mosel Spätlese 5 / 8 to 25 / 40, Auslese 8 / 10 to 35 / 50, GG 4 / 6 to 15 / 25. Rioja Reserva 5 / 7 to 18 / 30, Gran Reserva 8 / 12 to 30 / 50. Vintage Port 15 / 20 to 50 / 80. Madeira frasqueira 20 / 25 to 150 / 200. Tokaji Aszú (post-2013) 5 / 8 to 40 / 80, Eszencia 10 / 15 to 100 / 200. Hunter Semillon 5 / 8 to 20 / 30. Napa premium Cabernet 6 / 8 to 20 / 30.
- Producer style is stated per profile in general terms: traditional long maceration and large casks lengthen both the closed phase and the limit; modern, riper, new-oak styles open about three to five years sooner but rarely last longer.
- Defaults: base by style (red 2 / 3 to 8 / 12, white 1 / 1 to 4 / 7, sweet 3 / 5 to 15 / 25, fortified 0 / 1 to 10 / 25 and so on), scaled 1.3 for top national categories (DOCG, DOCa, DOQ, VP), 0.8 for broad ones (IGP, PGI, Vinho Regional, zones and super zones), 1.25 for cool-climate whites (Germany, Austria, Switzerland, New Zealand, Hungary and others) and 0.8 for young industries. The lookup order is written into `_meta.lookup`.
- Fine-unit rule (in `_meta.fine_units`): the profile is the appellation named before `-premier-cru-` or `-grand-cru-` in the id when that id is profiled (this corrects 668 spatial-parent glitches such as Pouilly-Fuissé premiers crus under Mâcon communes and Meursault premiers crus inside Blagny), else the first profiled id up the parent chain. The tier is `_fine_override[id]`, else the first candidate present in `_fine_tier['<profile>|<subcat>']`, else `_fine_tier['<country>|<subcat>']`, else the profile's default. Mappings: Barolo and Barbaresco MGAs to cru (236); Chianti Classico UGAs to gran_selezione; Alto Adige UGAs to white_top; Burgundy, Chablis and Côte Chalonnaise premiers crus to premier_cru or red_premier_cru; Corton and Charlemagne climats to grand_cru; lieux-dits to single-site tiers in Sancerre, Côte-Rôtie, Saint-Joseph, Châteauneuf (red_top), Moulin-à-Vent and Morgon (new climat tier), default elsewhere (Vouvray, Condrieu, Cornas, Hermitage, Fleurie, Savennières, Château-Grillet); Champagne cru villages, Rioja zonas and municipios, Cava zones and Mosel Einzellagen to their default; Priorat vi de vila to standard; Einzellagen elsewhere to GG; Rieden to riedenwein, ried, smaragd or Weinviertel reserve. Alto Adige subzones have id overrides (Santa Maddalena, Meranese, Colli di Bolzano to Schiava; Terlano to white_top). New profiles were added for Burgenland, Niederösterreich and Lago di Caldaro, and premier cru tiers for Pouilly-Loché and Pouilly-Vinzelles.

### Gaps
- No single source publishes ready, peak and limit numbers by tier; the figures are expert synthesis checked for internal consistency (ready ≤ peak ≤ limit, and vintage windows within about 1.35 times the reference tier's limit).
- CellarTracker community drinking-window data was not used: the site blocks automated access (HTTP 403) and no aggregate export was available.
- Michael Broadbent's *Vintage Wine* was not consulted (print only); older legendary vintages rely on widely repeated chart consensus.
- About 1,370 minor appellations (much of the Balkans, Romania, Bulgaria, Serbia and many US AVAs) use defaults only.

## (B) For about 40 regions, what is each vintage 1990 to 2024 like and where does it stand in October 2026?

### Takeaway
41 chart entries cover every requested region. Each row has a 1 to 5 quality band, a short character, a drinking window for a named reference tier, a status recomputed for October 2026 from that window, a short paraphrased note and a source URL. Recent years (2021 to 2024) were checked against 2025 and 2026 reports; older years follow chart consensus, cross-checked against Systembolaget's 1 to 5 ratings. Biggest recent swings: 2024 was a broad classic Port declaration, a difficult mildew year in Bordeaux and Burgundy, the smallest Mosel crop in fifty years, and "lots of grapes, little alcohol" in Barolo.

### Cited Findings
- Bordeaux 2024 was "pyramid-shaped": mildew before budbreak and uneven flowering; top wines ripe, fresh and moderate in alcohol, lesser wines lean; limestone Saint-Émilion, Margaux, Saint-Julien and Saint-Estèphe did best, Pauillac less so; dry whites a highlight; Sauternes uneven and often short of acidity. [The Drinks Business, Bordeaux 2024 in a nutshell](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2025/04/bordeaux-2024-in-a-nutshell-a-pyramid-shaped-vintage/)
- Burgundy 2024 was a struggle against nature in which diligence paid off (headline seen in search; article not fetched). [Wine Spectator Burgundy 2024 preview](https://www.winespectator.com/articles/2024-burgundy-wine-vintage-preview)
- Champagne 2024: downy mildew "ravages yields yet quality remains" (headline only). [Decanter, Champagne harvest 2024](https://www.decanter.com/wine-news/champagne-harvest-2024-downy-mildew-ravages-yields-yet-quality-remains-539707/). Champagne 2023: about 20,500 kg/ha, the third-largest crop ever, record 220 g bunches, mildew, botrytis and Drosophila suzukii; Chardonnay fared best. [The Wine Independent, 2023 Champagne harvest](https://thewineindependent.com/articles/2023-champagne-harvest-report/)
- Barolo 2022: yields down 30 to 40 percent, open aromatics, fine tannins at best, some wines over 15 percent; Serralunga most consistent; earlier drinking. Barbaresco 2023: mid-weight, riper and more polished tannins than 2022, uneven, hail in places, an August heatwave and an early harvest. [Jeroboams Piedmont report, March 2026](https://jeroboams.co.uk/2026/03/10/article/piedmont-vintage-report-Barolo-2022-Barbaresco-2023). 2021 Barolo and Barbaresco called exceptional. [Jeb Dunnuck, May 2025](https://jebdunnuck.com/2025/05/new-releases-from-barolo-and-barbaresco-exceptional-2021s-and-a-preview-of-2022/). Barolo 2024 "lots of grapes, little alcohol" (headline). [JancisRobinson.com](https://jancisrobinson.com/articles/barolo-harvest-report-2024-lots-grapes-little-alcohol)
- Rhône: 2024 an outlier with mixed results; Côte-Rôtie and Condrieu early drinking; Cornas charming. 2023 south "wines for immediate pleasure", Châteauneuf whites the ones to watch. 2022 Hermitage majestic, Châteauneuf the best-performing appellation, Côte-Rôtie needs time. 2019 Hermitage statuesque. - Port: 2024 declared by Symington (seven classic wines plus Roriz and Smith Woodhouse), Taylor's, Croft, Fonseca, Sandeman, Ferreira, Niepoort, Noval, Churchill's, Ramos Pinto and others; Noval called the conditions particularly favourable. ### Inferences
- Re-ageing rule (stored in `_meta.status_rule`): windows were set for October 2026 from current reports and the age implied by older charts, then status is computed purely from the window: before it is young; first 15 percent approachable; 15 to 50 percent peak; 50 to 80 percent mature (plateau); last 20 percent drink_up; after it past. A 2018 chart's "hold" for a 2010 Médoc therefore becomes "approachable" now, while a 2015 "drink" for 2007 Bordeaux becomes "drink_up". Totals: 438 past, 262 mature, 251 peak, 229 drink_up, 129 approachable, 124 young.
- Windows refer to each entry's `ref_tier` (stated in `_meta.entries[key].ref_label`, with the tier's profile numbers copied as `ref_window_profile`). For another tier, shift additively: from += tier.ready − ref.ready, to += tier.limit − ref.limit. Worked example: 2011 Barbaresco reference window 2016 to 2030 (mature); an MGA cru such as Oddero Gallina shifts to 2018 to 2038, which reads "peak" in October 2026.
- Region entry ids: `region-fr-medoc` (Left Bank, also covers Graves and Pessac-Léognan reds), `region-fr-libournais`, `region-fr-sauternais`, `region-fr-burgundy:red` and `:white` (colour-split keys; `_by_app` lists both for dual-colour villages), `region-fr-chablis-and-the-grand-auxerrois`, `region-fr-champagne`, `region-fr-northern-rhone`, `region-fr-southern-rhone`, `region-fr-loire-valley:chenin` and `:red`, `region-fr-alsace`, `region-fr-beaujolais`, `it-barolo`, `it-barbaresco`, `it-brunello-di-montalcino`, `it-chianti-classico`, `it-amarone-della-valpolicella`, `it-etna`, `es-rioja`, `es-ribera-del-duero`, `es-priorat`, `pt-porto`, `pt-madeira`, `de-mosel`, `de-rheingau` (covers the Nahe), `at-wachau` (covers Kremstal and Kamptal), `hu-tokaj`, `us-napa-valley`, `us-russian-river-valley` (Sonoma Pinot), `us-willamette-valley`, `us-columbia-valley`, `au-barossa-valley`, `au-margaret-river`, `au-coonawarra`, `au-hunter-valley` (Semillon), `nz-marlborough`, `nz-central-otago`, `za-stellenbosch`, `ar-mendoza`, `cl-valle-del-maipo` (covers Colchagua and Cachapoal). Region membership comes from the content files' `regions[].app_ids` and, for New World nests, the index parent chain.
- Vintage Port rows in undeclared years carry single-quinta windows (stated in the notes); Madeira rows refer to specific documented bottlings, because house and cask matter more than the harvest.
- Notes are my paraphrase only, 7 to 43 words (median 17); at the coordinator's request there is no minimum length and no generated window sentence, because the window is shown separately.

### Gaps
- Deliberately shorter series (no reliable year-by-year source found): Etna from 2013, Priorat from 1995, Hunter Semillon and Stellenbosch from 2000, Sonoma Pinot from 2000, Mendoza from 2002, Central Otago from 2002, Chile from 2001, Marlborough from 2005, Tokaj 1993 and 1999 to 2023 (no 2024 yet). Madeira lists 11 notable years only.
- Year-specific weather for the 1990s in Ribera, Priorat, Margaret River, Coonawarra and Mendoza is thin; those notes describe the chart band and current state rather than inventing weather.
- 2024 Northern Hemisphere reds (Barolo, Brunello, Amarone, Rioja Reserva) are not yet released; their windows rest on harvest reports.
- JancisRobinson.com and several trade sites block automated fetching; some 2024 points rest on headlines seen in search results (marked above). 2025 Northern Hemisphere vintages are not included (outside scope); Southern Hemisphere 2025 rows come from Systembolaget only.
- The 1 to 5 bands are my paraphrased synthesis across charts; no critic scores or text are reproduced.

## How do the two datasets join to the atlas ids, and how reliable are the links?

### Takeaway
All ids were checked against `site/data/geo/_index.json` and `_regions.geojson` at build time (0 unknown ids). Of 89 distinct source URLs, 71 returned HTTP 200 to a scripted check; the rest are bot-blocks or transient failures on pages that were fetched or seen in search during this research.

### Cited Findings
- Index structure: 11,833 entries, of which 2,581 are main appellations and the rest fine units with `fine: 1` and a `parent`. Regions are listed in `_regions.geojson` with ids like `region-fr-burgundy`, and their member appellations sit in `data/content/{iso2}.json` `regions[].app_ids`. [Vitis Mundi data contract](file:///home/claude/winesite/DATA_CONTRACT.md)
- URL check results per URL are in `work/ageing/url_check.tsv`. The official consortium domains for Barolo and Barbaresco (langhevini.it) and Chianti Classico (chianticlassico.com) did not resolve from the sandbox on the day; they are cited as the bodies' official sites. [url_check.tsv](file:///home/claude/winesite/work/ageing/url_check.tsv)

### Inferences
- Main-appellation `parent` links in the index are spatial containment, not legal hierarchy (for example `it-barbaresco` has parent `it-dolcetto-d-alba`, and a glitch puts `nz-new-zealand` under `nz-marlborough`), so parent-walking is used only for fine units (with the id rule above taking precedence for premiers and grands crus), plus explicit, checked New World nests (Napa, Willamette, Columbia Valley, Stellenbosch, Mendoza, Maipo and Rapel).
- Profiles and vintages are linked through `_meta.entries[key].profile` and `ref_tier`; the vintage build refuses to run if either is missing.

### Gaps
- Brunello single vineyards are not in the geo index, so its cru tier cannot be auto-selected; one Austrian Ried has no parent and uses the country default.
- `profiles.json` repeats family texts across hundreds of ids (1.1 MB uncompressed; it gzips well). Consider lazy-loading it by country.
